# Credits

unslop is an original synthesis. The work below shaped it.

## Emil Kowalski: architecture and motion craft

The audit, plan, execute pattern (an expensive model plans, a cheap model executes, the audit is read-only, plans are self-contained) and the motion thresholds behind the detector's section G are adapted from Emil Kowalski's design-engineering skills.

- Skills: https://github.com/emilkowalski/skills (MIT License, © Emil Kowalski). The license notice is in [LICENSES/emilkowalski-skills-MIT.txt](LICENSES/emilkowalski-skills-MIT.txt).
- Writing: https://emilkowal.ski/
- For deep motion work, compose unslop with his `improve-animations` skill.

## impeccable.style: slop taxonomy

Their anti-pattern taxonomy informed several rule categories, including DEC-2, DEC-3, DEC-6, DEC-7, DEC-8 and DEC-9. unslop states the ideas in its own words and does not redistribute their content.

- https://impeccable.style/slop/
- Their `impeccable detect` CLI can run alongside unslop as a separate detector.

## Jakub Krehel: invisible interface details

Rules KR-1, KR-3, KR-3d, KR-4, KR-5, KR-7, KR-8, KR-9, KR-10, KR-11, KR-13, KR-15 and KR-16 are adapted, in unslop's own words, from his article and skill on the details that make interfaces feel better.

- Writing: https://jakub.kr/writing/details-that-make-interfaces-feel-better
- Skill: https://github.com/jakubkrehel/make-interfaces-feel-better

## invisibledetails.com: speed by the weight of the action

The free notes "Small details that add up" are the second source of KR-16 (feedback fast, context changes slower). Only the open notes were used, not the paid course.

- https://invisibledetails.com

## Benji Taylor: morphing icons

NR-02 (icon transitions rotate rather than morph between shapes of one form) follows his post "Morphing icons with Claude".

- https://benji.org

## Nick DiLallo: false urgency

LK-01 (no false urgency in interface copy) follows his article "This is good UX writing" (UX Collective).

- https://uxdesign.cc/this-is-good-ux-writing-10c4b956a6c3

## Refactoring UI: fewer borders

DEC-2 (lines only where structure needs them) follows the advice "Use fewer borders" by Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger.

- https://www.refactoringui.com/

## Aurora Harley (NN/g): common region

DEC-2 also draws on the observation that borders are often added out of caution, from the Nielsen Norman Group article "Common Region".

- https://www.nngroup.com/articles/common-region/

## AINDF

The token and design-system rules follow the AI-Native Design Framework: https://github.com/0design/ai-native-design-framework
